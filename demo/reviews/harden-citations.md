# Citation verification — 1949 poliovirus serotype literature

**Reviewer role:** Independent fresh-context citation researcher (read-only). No
prior project context used; claims checked against live authoritative sources
(PubMed, Oxford Academic archive, journal-history sources). Where a fact could
not be confirmed it is marked UNVERIFIED rather than guessed.

**Date of verification:** 2026-07-10

---

## Bottom line

- The 1949 series exists and is a **three-part** series in **American Journal of
  Hygiene, vol. 49, issue 2** (1949). All three parts confirmed on PubMed.
- The source email's journal name — **"American Journal of Epidemiology"** — is
  **anachronistic for 1949**. The journal was named *American Journal of Hygiene*
  until 1965, when it was renamed *American Journal of Epidemiology*. Volume 49
  (1949) was an *AJH* volume. (Modern databases and the Oxford Academic archive
  now host these under the AJE brand, which is the likely origin of the error.)
- "Bodian 1949" as the single paper establishing three serotypes is a **defensible
  but slightly loose** attribution — see Claim 1 nuance below.

## Claim 1 — "Bodian 1949 first made clear there are three serotypes"

**Verified citations (this is a 3-part series, not one paper):**

- **Part I** — Bodian D. "Differentiation of types of poliomyelitis viruses;
  reinfection experiments in monkeys (second attacks)." *Am J Hyg.* 1949;49(2):200-223.
  PMID **18113219**. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18113219/
- **Part II** — Morgan IM. "Differentiation of types of poliomyelitis viruses;
  by reciprocal vaccination-immunity experiments." *Am J Hyg.* 1949;49(2):225-233.
  PMID **18124443**. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18124443/
- **Part III** — Bodian D, Morgan IM, Howe HA. "Differentiation of types of
  poliomyelitis viruses; the grouping of 14 strains into three basic immunological
  types." *Am J Hyg.* 1949;49(2):234-245. PMID **18113220**.
  URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18113220/

**Authorship:** Confirmed. The paper that actually groups strains into three types
(Part III) is Bodian, Morgan **& Howe** — three authors, not "et al." beyond that.
Part I is Bodian alone; Part II is Morgan alone.

**Year:** 1949 — confirmed (high).

**Journal + volume:** *American Journal of Hygiene*, vol. 49, issue 2 — confirmed
by PubMed (high). NOT "American Journal of Epidemiology" for a 1949 imprint.

**Does it establish/formalize three antigenic types?** Yes — Part III explicitly
groups 14 strains into three basic immunological types. Confidence **high** that
this series formalized the three-type scheme.

**Nuance on "first" (confidence: medium):**
- Antigenic *differences* between polioviruses were first shown by **Burnet &
  Macnamara (1931)**, who distinguished two immunological types. So "first paper
  to show poliovirus has antigenic types" belongs to 1931, not Bodian.
- The definitive, large-scale confirmation that there are **exactly three** types
  was the **NFIP Committee on Typing** program (~100 strains, results published
  ~1951, *Am J Hyg* vol. 54). Some histories credit the three-type settlement to
  that committee "in the early 1950s" rather than to Bodian 1949.
- Fair characterization: **Bodian, Morgan & Howe 1949 is the paper that first
  established/formalized the three-type classification** (grouping 14 strains into
  three types) and directly seeded the NFIP typing program. Calling it "the first
  paper that made clear there are three serotypes" is defensible if you mean the
  three-type *scheme*; it slightly overstates if read as "first to show poliovirus
  has multiple antigenic types" (that's Burnet 1931) or as the definitive
  confirmation (that's the 1951 committee work).

**Discrepancies vs. the wording provided:**
1. Journal name: "American Journal of Epidemiology" → should be **American Journal
   of Hygiene** for 1949.
2. "Bodian 1949" collapses a 3-part series into one paper; the strain-grouping
   result is specifically **Part III (Bodian, Morgan & Howe)**.
3. Minor page-range note: one secondary web source listed Part III as pp. 234-**247**;
   PubMed gives **234-245**. Trust PubMed (234-245); flag if the exact end page matters.

## Claim 2 — "Morgan 1949" companion paper on cross-protection / cross-immunity

**Verified citation:**
- Morgan IM. "Differentiation of types of poliomyelitis viruses; by reciprocal
  vaccination-immunity experiments." *Am J Hyg.* 1949;49(2):225-233.
  PMID **18124443**. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18124443/

**Topic confirmed (high):** The title itself — *reciprocal vaccination-immunity
experiments* — is exactly cross-immunity / cross-protection testing between strains,
i.e. the companion cross-protection paper. Same journal, same volume/issue (AJH
49(2)), sole author Isabel Morgan. This matches the description of a companion
paper used for cross-protection / cross-immunity data.

**"Kover" strain / symmetric cross-protection: UNVERIFIED.** I could not confirm a
poliovirus strain named **"Kover"** in any indexed source, nor the specific
"symmetric cross-protection" finding attached to it. This may be a strain name
internal to the primary paper (not surfaced in abstracts/indexes), a transcription
variant of another strain name, or an error. Recommend checking the full text of
Morgan 1949 (Part II) directly before citing "Kover." Do not assert the Kover
detail as verified.

## Claim 3 — Did the 1949 AJH vol. 49 literature discuss cross-immunity /
## heterotypic responses / strains intermediate between types?

**Cross-immunity / heterotypic responses: confirmed (high) that the issue covers
this.** Two of the three parts are explicitly about it:
- Part I (Bodian) — *reinfection experiments in monkeys (second attacks)*: this is
  heterotypic reinfection / cross-protection between types.
- Part II (Morgan) — *reciprocal vaccination-immunity experiments*: cross-immunity
  between strains/types.
So the surrounding AJH vol. 49(2) series did address cross-immunity and heterotypic
response, by title and by design.

**Strains "intermediate between types": UNVERIFIED / low confidence.** I found no
indexed statement that the 1949 series described strains intermediate between the
three types. Abstracts/indexes do not surface this. It is plausible the primary
texts note imperfect/asymmetric cross-reactions, but I cannot confirm an explicit
"intermediate strain" claim from live sources. Verify against full text before
asserting.
