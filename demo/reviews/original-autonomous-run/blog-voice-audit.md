## Verdict

Faithful to the casual register. The piece reads as a personal-scientific essay,
not a neutral explainer: mechanism stays in view while the author is emotionally
exposed, conjecture is labeled plainly (and if anything over-labeled), and the
moral/mechanistic stakes are joined at the close. No drift into the formal-scientific
sibling register. The findings below are refinements and author-decision candidates,
not fidelity failures.

## Preserved-voice examples

- Casual reframe pivots: "So I went back to the tables." / "Here's the thing that got me."
- MEF-1 texture kept concrete: "literally 'Middle East Forces,' from a 1942 outbreak among British soldiers."
- Per strain kept with its human weight (three-year-old girl, West Virginia, 1940, Sabin) rather than abstracted to a data point.
- Conjecture kept honest under scrutiny: "Per could be exactly that kind of virus" (not "Per is").
- Noise self-check preserved: "Could it be noise? I can't rule out sampling variation or lab error. But… a 2-of-6 where you'd expect 6-of-6 is weird."

## Drift examples

None rising to fidelity failure. The two mild candidates above (softened emotional
disclosure; possibly-forced "almost funny") are the only phrase-level items that read
as smoothing/insertion rather than authorial choice.

The register is correctly casual, not neutral-explainer.
