## Central claim
The three-serotype model of poliovirus is treated as a hard, clean partition, but the
pre-1950s literature measured real cross-immunity between "types." Later assay and
reference-strain standardization largely defined that evidence out of routine view. The
conditions that may once have let off-type variants persist (heterogeneous population
immunity) are being recreated by type-2 OPV cessation, so between-serotype viruses should
be expected rather than surprising. This does not imply that the categories themselves
were manufactured or are biologically unreal.

## Subclaims
1. Pre-standardization virologists already saw serotype fuzziness (cross-immunity across
   many monkeys).
2. Bodian 1949 fixed the three-serotype scheme and the type-defining challenge pattern.
3. Specific strains violated the clean pattern: **Per** (grouped type 1 but cross-protected
   by type 2) and **Kotter** (symmetric cross-protection, seen in both Bodian and Morgan 1949).
4. Standardization (early 1950s: cell culture + reference strains) operationally erased the
   ambiguity; most named isolates dropped out — only 4 of 14 survived to be sequenced.
5. Evolutionary reading: heterogeneous cross-serotype immunity selects for/permits off-type
   persistence; trivalent vaccination synchronized immunity and suppressed it.
6. Modern WPV is under strong purifying selection, tiny antigenic drift, VP1-only typing →
   off-type variants effectively invisible.
7. OPV2 cessation re-heterogenizes population immunity → off-type ("between serotype")
   viruses become plausible again.

## Evidence (and its grade)
- Historical experimental tables (Bodian 1949, Morgan 1949): small-N monkey challenge data.
  Author is explicit it's "old data and small N."
- Author's cross-domain support: field experience of bOPV cross-protection against type 2;
  a colleague's review of early literature + modern enterovirus cross-reactivity work.
- Author's quantitative asides (Cairo R0 ~10x, virulence ~4 orders of magnitude, titer
  factors 4–8): stated as recollection/observation, not formally sourced.

## Assumptions (author's, stated as such)
- "Between-type" viruses are less intrinsically fit but persist transiently under immunity
  heterogeneity.
- ~5% aa distance between serotypes is largely neutral/historical, so cross-over is not as
  hard as the distance implies.

"all the permutations are possible in principle", "should not be surprising" (calibrated,
not "proves").
